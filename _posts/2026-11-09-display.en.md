The screens are one of _Sharly Chess_'s flagship features, but setting them up was not always easy: display controllers few people knew about, multi-screens that could not be sent to a projector, results entry screens that duplicated the pairings screens, and nothing to look at before the first round. Many of you told us so — thank you for the feedback!

Version 5.3 of _Sharly Chess_ rethinks how the screens are set up. The main changes are:

- a single **Display** section, which opens on a **Guide** explaining how it all fits together
- **channels**, which let you drive any device from the arbiter's computer
- screens on **one page or over several pages**, in one place
- **result entry** on any pairings screen, including the pairings by player
- **fake data**, to set up the screens before the tournament starts
- a **Display page** for players and spectators
- a **round-robin grid** screen

## One Display section, and a Guide

The side menu now has a single **Display** entry. Its tabs run along the top of the page: **Guide**, **Channels**, **Screens**, **Rotators**, **Menus** and **Timers**. As long as the event has nothing to display, the Display entry opens the Guide; after that, it opens the last display tab you used.

The **Guide**, headed _Your tournaments, on all your devices_, explains how to show pairings, rankings, results and check-in anywhere in the venue. On the left, you choose a setup in three steps:

1. **the screen**: one page with several tournaments, or one tournament over several pages;
2. **what the device opens**: a screen, a rotator, a menu, or a channel whose content the arbiter changes at any time;
3. **the devices**: a projector or a monitor plugged into this computer, with no network needed, or any tablet, television or computer on the same network, which opens _Sharly Chess_ from this computer's network address or its QR code.

An optional timer can be added on top. On the right, an example plays out your choices on drawings of the devices: pages turning by themselves, a rotator going through its screens, a menu being tapped, or a channel driving a projector and tablets from the arbiter's computer. It only illustrates the idea, with made-up tournaments. Below it, a button creates each kind of item: a channel, a screen, a rotator, a menu or a timer.

Each display tab, while it is empty, explains in one sentence what its items are for, with a link back to the Guide.

👉 Read the new [Display]({% link docs/screens/index.en.md %}) page.

## Channels

**Channels** replace the display controllers. A device opens a channel once, and from then on you decide from the arbiter's computer what the channel shows. Every device following the channel changes together.

A channel is not a way of connecting a device: a projector plugged into the arbiter's computer can follow a channel just as well as a tablet on the network, and driving the projector of the playing hall through a channel is a common setup. Channels remain optional: without one, a device simply opens a screen, a rotator or a menu, and what it shows is changed on the device.

- A channel can now show anything: a screen, a screen over several pages, which goes through its pages, a single page of it, a rotator, or a menu that the players use on a tablet.
- Mark your screens, rotators and menus as **Designed for** a channel, such as _Projector_ or _Tablets_, and they come first when choosing what the channel shows. They then carry a badge with the channel's name wherever they are listed or chosen.
- The **Channels** tab can show a live preview of every channel, to check all the devices of the playing hall from the arbiter's table.

👉 See [Channels]({% link docs/screens/display-controllers.en.md %}).

## One page or several pages

Multi-screens are now screens over several pages, also called **paged screens**, and they are listed with the other screens on the **Screens** tab. When you create a screen, a switch at the top of the window chooses between **one or more tournaments, on one page**, and **one tournament, over several pages**.

- A screen can be given **several tournaments** as soon as it is created.
- A screen over several pages sets how many **seconds each page** is shown, on a channel or in a rotator.
- Opened directly, a screen over several pages **goes through its pages** instead of showing the first one.
- Ranking screens let you **choose their columns**, to make room for the players on a long ranking, and always say after which round they rank, such as _After round 5_.
- The **Screens** tab has a new **preview** view, where screens over several pages are drawn as a stack of pages.

👉 See [Screens]({% link docs/screens/single-screens.en.md %}) and [One tournament over several pages]({% link docs/screens/single-screens.en.md %}#paged-screens).

## Result entry on the pairings screens

The **Results entry** screen type is gone. Instead, the pairings by board and pairings by player screens have a switch to **accept results**, and the check-in screens a switch to let players **check in**.

- Players can now enter their result from the **pairings by player** screen: they tap their own name, which opens the result of their board.
- A screen accepting input carries a **Result entry** or **Check-in** badge, and one that could take input but does not carries a **View only** badge.
- Screens that do not accept input can be added to **rotators**, whatever their type. A screen over several pages that accepts input stays on its page, so it has no seconds per page.
- Results and check-ins are only taken by the screens set to accept them.

Your existing results entry screens become pairings by board screens accepting results, and your check-in screens keep accepting check-ins.

👉 See [Accepting input]({% link docs/screens/single-screens.en.md %}#accepting-input).

## Fake data

Before the first round, a pairings or ranking screen has nothing to show, which makes it hard to choose its layout. The new **Fake data** button fills the screens of the tournaments that have not started with a made-up first round, paired as the tournament's pairing system would pair it, with a result on every board.

The fake data is shown on every device, so that you can check the screens where they will really be seen, and each screen showing it says so. Nothing is saved, and the tournaments already paired keep their real data.

👉 See [Setting up the screens before the tournament starts]({% link docs/screens/index.en.md %}#fake-data).

## A Display page for visitors

Players and spectators connected to the network now find a **Display** page, without the administration around it. It starts with the public channels — choosing one opens it straight away — and lists the public screens, menus and rotators, each with a preview.

👉 See [The Display page for visitors]({% link docs/screens/index.en.md %}#visitors).

## Round-robin grids

Round robins get a screen of their own: the **round-robin grid**, every player against every other, or every team. The squares show the results of the games played and, for those to come, the round they are played in, so players can see at a glance when they meet whom. On a grid of players, each square is coloured white or black for the colour the player of its row has in that game, and split in two in a double round robin. The grid is ordered by pairing number or by rank, and a team grid can show a row per player.

👉 See [Round-robin grids]({% link docs/screens/single-screens.en.md %}#round-robin-grids).

## Menus, rotators and timers

- A **menu** has its own address, and can be sent to a channel. A screen can belong to several menus.
- Menus, rotators and timers choose their screens from **a single list**, grouped by screen type, where each screen shows its channel and page badges.
- New menus, rotators and timers are named _Menu_, _Rotator_ and _Timer_, then _Menu (2)_ and so on, and a new channel starts with an empty name and a few examples to inspire you.
- The pairings by player screen now waits for the tournament to start, as the pairings by board screen does.
