The screens are one of _Sharly Chess_'s flagship features, but setting them up was not always easy: display controllers few people knew about, multi-screens that could not be sent to a projector, results entry screens that duplicated the pairings screens, and nothing to look at before the first round. Many of you told us so — thank you for the feedback!

Version 5.3 of _Sharly Chess_ rethinks how the screens are set up. The main changes are:

- a single **Display** section, with one tab for each part of the setup
- **feeds**, which let you drive any device from the arbiter's computer
- screens and **paged screens** in one place
- **result entry** on any pairings screen, including the pairings by player
- **fake data**, to set up the screens before the tournament starts
- a **Display page** for players and spectators
- a **round-robin grid** screen

## One Display section

The side menu now has a single **Display** entry. Its tabs, along the top of the page, follow the order in which you usually work: **Feeds**, **Screens**, **Rotators**, **Menus** and **Timers**. A diagram, opened from the tab bar, shows how they fit together and when to use each of them.

## Feeds

**Feeds** replace the display controllers. Open a feed once on a device — by typing its address or by scanning its QR code — and from then on, choose what the device shows from the arbiter's computer. Any number of devices can follow the same feed.

A feed can now show anything: a screen, a paged screen, which goes through its pages, a single page of a paged screen, a rotator, or a menu that the players use on a tablet.

Mark your screens as **Designed for** a feed, such as _Projector_ or _Tablets_, and they come first when choosing what the feed shows. The **Feeds** tab can also show a live preview of every feed, to check all the devices of the playing hall from the arbiter's table.

👉 See [Feeds]({% link docs/screens/display-controllers.en.md %}).

## Screens and paged screens

Multi-screens are now called **paged screens**, and they are listed with the other screens on the **Screens** tab. When you create a screen, a switch at the top of the window chooses between **one or more tournaments, on one page**, and **one tournament, over several pages**.

- A screen can be given **several tournaments** as soon as it is created.
- A paged screen sets how many **seconds each page** is shown, on a feed or in a rotator.
- Opened directly, a paged screen **goes through its pages** instead of showing the first one.
- Ranking screens let you **choose their columns**, to make room for the players on a long ranking.
- The **Screens** tab has a new **preview** view, where paged screens are drawn as a stack of pages.

## Result entry on the pairings screens

The **Results entry** screen type is gone. Instead, the pairings by board and pairings by player screens have a switch to **accept results**, and the check-in screens a switch to let players **check in**.

- Players can now enter their result from the **pairings by player** screen: they tap their own name, which opens the result of their board.
- Screens that do not accept input can be added to **rotators**, whatever their type.
- Results and check-ins are only taken by the screens set to accept them.

Your existing results entry screens become pairings by board screens accepting results, and your check-in screens keep accepting check-ins.

👉 See [Accepting input]({% link docs/screens/single-screens.en.md %}#accepting-input).

## Fake data

Before the first round, a pairings or ranking screen has nothing to show, which makes it hard to choose its layout. The new **Fake data** button fills the screens of the tournaments that have not started with a made-up first round, paired as the tournament's pairing system would pair it, with a result on every board.

The fake data is shown on every device, so that you can check the screens where they will really be seen, and each screen showing it says so. Nothing is saved, and the tournaments already paired keep their real data.

👉 See [Setting up the screens before the tournament starts]({% link docs/screens/index.en.md %}#fake-data).

## A Display page for visitors

Players and spectators connected to the network now find a **Display** page, without the administration around it. It starts with the public feeds — choosing one opens it straight away — and lists the public screens, menus and rotators, each with a preview.

👉 See [The Display page for visitors]({% link docs/screens/index.en.md %}#visitors).

## Round-robin grids

Round robins get a screen of their own: the **round-robin grid**, every player against every other, or every team. The squares show the results of the games played and, for those to come, the round they are played in, so players can see at a glance when they meet whom. The grid is ordered by pairing number or by rank, and a team grid can show a row per player.

👉 See [Round-robin grids]({% link docs/screens/single-screens.en.md %}#round-robin-grids).

## Menus and rotators

- A **menu** has its own address, and can be sent to a feed. A screen can belong to several menus.
- Menus and rotators choose their screens and paged screens from **a single list**.
- The pairings by player screen now waits for the tournament to start, as the pairings by board screen does.
